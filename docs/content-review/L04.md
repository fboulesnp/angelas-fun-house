## Review flags and resolutions
- **L04-RF1** (pp. 42, 47, 48)
  - Issue: OCR is unusable on the opener and garbles the photosynthesis equation and the Key Science Concept labels. The p. 47 text also calls the equation a "chemical formula".
  - Resolution: read from the renders (Figure 2B also at 220 dpi). The equation is 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂, with 6 C, 12 H and 18 O on each side. Content says "chemical equation" for the whole reaction and "formula" for each substance.
  - Sources: textbook pp. 47–48 (rendered).
- **L04-RF2** (pp. 43, 46–48, 50, 53)
  - Issue: energy is described as "stored in chemical bonds", and cells "release the energy stored in the bonds". Taken literally, that contradicts the guardrail that breaking bonds requires energy.
  - Resolution: content says light energy ends up stored as chemical energy in sugar and is released when sugar reacts with oxygen. The O2 watchOut explains the shorthand, and no question asks where in a molecule energy sits.
  - Sources: RSC Education, "Chemical energetics: words matter". OpenStax Biology 2e 8.1 shows the shorthand is common.
- **L04-RF3** (p. 46)
  - Issue: the text says chlorophyll does not absorb green light. In fact, leaves absorb about 70–80 percent of green light (about 90 percent of red and blue), and absorbed green light drives photosynthesis.
  - Resolution: Q07 is keyed to "absorbs green light weakly". Q08 predicts that a green-lit seedling still makes some sugar. The O1 watchOut covers it.
  - Source: The University of Tokyo, "Why are leaves green?" (Terashima).
- **L04-RF4** (pp. 49–50)
  - Issue: the Figure 3 labels can be read as "plants give off CO₂ only in the dark". Brief guardrail: plants photosynthesize and respire.
  - Resolution: Q15 teaches that sugar breakdown continues day and night and that photosynthesis outpaces it in bright light. The O3 watchOut covers it.
  - Sources: Encyclopedia of the Environment (Dizengremel, Université de Lorraine); Botany online (University of Hamburg); textbook p. 50.
- **L04-RF5** (p. 49)
  - Issue: the textbook's "about 70 percent from carbon dioxide, most of the rest from water" for Saussure does not match modern dry-mass estimates. Those put about 90 percent (carbon plus oxygen) as tracing to CO₂, about 6 percent hydrogen from water, and about 4 percent soil minerals.
  - Resolution: the percentage is never tested. Q16 says "most" and defines dry mass.
  - Sources: Carbon TIME (Michigan State University); Botany online (University of Hamburg; also van Helmont's willow).
- **L04-RF6** (pp. 43, 51)
  - Issue: p. 51 says "all other organisms" depend on photosynthesis, while p. 43 says "nearly all". Chemosynthetic vent communities are the exception.
  - Resolution: content says "nearly all", and the O4 watchOut names the exception. Q23 uses a single grassland chain.
  - Source: NOAA Ocean Exploration, photosynthesis vs chemosynthesis.
- **L04-RF7** (p. 45)
  - Issue: the text says cyanobacteria live in "boiling-hot" springs. Their growth limit is about 70–74 °C.
  - Resolution: not tested, and content avoids "boiling".
  - Source: University of Edinburgh, The Microbial World (Deacon), Yellowstone thermophiles.
- **L04-RF8** (pp. 52–53)
  - Issue: the biofuel section is dated or simplified:
    - It calls jet biofuel "low-cost". Today it costs several times more than conventional jet fuel and is under 1 percent of use.
    - It says sugars become "alcohol that is used as fuel". In practice, alcohols are upgraded into jet-fuel hydrocarbons and blended with conventional fuel.
    - It uses "efficient" for energy per unit mass.
  - Resolution: questions test the criteria, constraints and trade-off reasoning, not market claims. Q26 carries a limitation with today's cost and share, content uses "energy per kilogram", and Q27 repairs "biofuel releases no CO₂" (the textbook itself says it does).
  - Sources: IATA SAF page; U.S. DOE Alternative Fuels Data Center (SAF); U.S. DOE Alternative Fuels and Feedstocks Office (SAF).
- **L04-RF9** (p. 50)
  - Issue: cellulose "looks like many sugar molecules bound together". Each link releases a water molecule.
  - Resolution: content says "linked sugar units". The Q21 explanation and the O4 watchOut mention the water released at each link.
  - Source: OpenStax Biology 2e 3.2 Carbohydrates.
- Not flagged:
  - The p. 45 statements that phytoplankton (including diatoms and cyanobacteria) photosynthesize near the sunlit surface are consistent with NASA Earth Observatory "What are Phytoplankton?" (opened). No external record was needed, because Q05 rests on the textbook page.
  - Historical dates (Ingenhousz late 1700s, Saussure early 1800s) match Botany online. They are used only in the O3 card, not tested.
