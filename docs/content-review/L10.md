## Review flags and resolutions
- **L10-RF1** (pp. 129–130)
  - Issue: the core is called a "rock layer", and it is said to be hottest because of pressure.
  - Resolution: content calls the core mostly iron-nickel metal. The pressure-causes-heat claim is not used anywhere. Q03 tests only the density order.
  - Sources: National Geographic, Core.
- **L10-RF2** (pp. 133, 139)
  - Issue: the ocean loop is explained by temperature alone.
  - Resolution: content adds salinity. Q18 keys both "chilled by frigid air" and "becomes saltier as sea ice forms".
  - Sources: NOAA Currents tutorial (Global Conveyor Belt); NOAA conveyor fact page.
- **L10-RF3** (pp. 136, 139)
  - Issue: the text says energy is "cycled" in the biosphere, and Figure 4 labels the decomposer-to-producer arrow "energy and matter".
  - Resolution: content teaches that matter cycles and energy flows through and leaves as heat. Q23 repairs that exact arrow label.
  - Sources: OpenStax Biology 2e 46.2 and 46.3.
- **L10-RF4** (pp. 136, 139)
  - Issue: "energy in the biosphere originates with the sun" is stated as an absolute.
  - Resolution: content says "most" or "nearly all". Q22 is framed as "in most food chains", with the vent exception in its limitations.
  - Sources: OpenStax 46.2 (chemoautotrophs).
- **L10-RF5** (p. 134)
  - Issue: carbon dioxide and water vapor are called "elements". The percentages are dry-air values and are dated.
  - Resolution: no question tests trace-gas percentages. Q07 tests only "mostly nitrogen and oxygen" and "weather in the lowest, densest air".
  - Sources: NOAA JetStream, The Atmosphere.
- **L10-RF6** (pp. 138–139)
  - Issue: Biosphere 2 was "closed" only to matter, not energy. The oxygen story is simplified (concrete absorbed CO₂, and oxygen was later pumped in). The facility figures and "today" date from when the textbook was written.
  - Resolution: content defines a closed system as closed to matter but open to energy. Q26 asks what could still cross the walls (sunlight, heat). Q27 keys bacteria, as the textbook does. Q28 asks for the best hypothesis about the missing CO₂. Acreage and species counts are not tested.
  - Sources: EARTH Magazine; Scientific American.
- **L10-RF7** (pp. 132, 137)
  - Issue: system thickness values are rough. The biosphere is given as "more than 70 km" (National Geographic says about 20 km), and the atmosphere has no sharp top.
  - Resolution: no question tests these thicknesses. Q05 uses only the crust and mantle values, which match standard figures.
  - Sources: National Geographic, Biosphere and Mantle.
- **L10-RF8** (p. 132)
  - Issue: the textbook gives fresh water as 3% of Earth's water; USGS gives 2.5%. The ice/groundwater/surface split agrees.
  - Resolution: Q06 asks only the ranking (most fresh water is in glaciers and ice caps).
  - Sources: USGS, Where is Earth's Water?
- **L10-RF9** (pp. 130–131)
  - Issue: the cake-batter analogy and the conduction-only wording for outward energy flow can suggest a liquid, fast-moving mantle.
  - Resolution: content says the mantle is solid rock that creeps over millions of years. Q04 repairs the "liquid magma mantle" idea, and Q25 names the speed and state difference as the key limit of the pot model.
  - Sources: National Geographic, Mantle.
- **L10-RF10** (p. 133)
  - Issue: "warm water is less dense than cold water" holds for fresh water only above about 4 °C.
  - Resolution: the seed question Q10 states its conditions, and the O2 explorer watch-out names the exception.
  - Sources: USGS, Water Density.
- **L10-RF11** (pp. 128, 129, 131, 135–137)
  - Issue: OCR is empty or garbled on these pages.
  - Resolution: every page was rendered, and the diagram facts used in content were taken from the renders.
