## Review flags and resolutions
- L01-RF1: pp. 9, 10 and 16 give exactly 92 naturally occurring elements, but the count depends on convention (Seaborg argued for 94 because of trace plutonium). → Content says "fewer than a hundred"; no exact count is tested (brief requirement). Round 1: the issue text now says technetium and promethium occur only as tiny fission traces (not "essentially absent"). (Scientific American; RSC Periodic Table pages for technetium and promethium)
- L01-RF2: the vocabulary box (p. 9) and p. 10 define an element as a "unique particle", which blurs it with an atom. → Element = substance of only one kind of atom (the p. 16 wording); atom = one particle of an element. (OpenStax Chemistry 2e §1.2)
- L01-RF3: pp. 9 and 14 say all atoms of an element have the same atomic mass, which ignores isotopes. → The glossary calls the table value an average; no question asserts identical masses; the O3 card names isotopes. Match distractor r5 in Q21 targets the claim. (OpenStax §2.3)
- L01-RF4: pp. 15, 18 and 19 describe the table as ordered by atomic mass. The modern table is ordered by atomic number, and the spread itself puts Ar 39.9 before K 39.1 and Te 127.6 before I 126.9. → Questions keep "mostly increasing, with a few exceptions". The O4 card explains Ar/K through properties; round 1 moved Q27 to Te/I so the card no longer gives Q27's answer, and Q20 now says mass "generally" increased. Verifier correction: Mendeleev's 1871 table (p. 15) lists Te=125 before J=127, which is mass order by his own values, so the page does not show the pair "placed by properties"; the flag's resolution now says this. (OpenStax §2.5; NIH PubChem)
- L01-RF5: pp. 13 and 19 say the STM let people see atoms "for the first time" and "proved" atoms exist. → No question claims either. The STM is presented as a 1981 tool that maps surfaces atom by atom; the O2 card names the 1955 field ion microscope. Round 1: the O2 card's constraint sentence and Q11 item i3 no longer say "no existing technology" could show atoms; they say the available tools could not map the atoms of an ordinary surface. (Penn State Eberly College of Science; NobelPrize.org 1986 press release)
- L01-RF6: the p. 12 caption says "Greg Binnig". → Gerd Binnig is used throughout. (NobelPrize.org)
- L01-RF7: the p. 11 Grand Canyon comparison (500,000 fills) is off by roughly a thousandfold. A 40 mm ball is about 3.4 × 10⁻⁵ m³, so 5 × 10¹⁹ balls take up about 1.7 × 10¹⁵ m³. Even a generous canyon-sized box (447 × 28.8 × 1.6 km ≈ 2.1 × 10¹³ m³) would be filled only about 80 times, or about 150 times at the 16 km average width. → The comparison is not used anywhere. The sand-grain count is kept only as a rough, size-dependent estimate (Q10). (NPS Grand Canyon park statistics; Table Tennis England Laws, 40 mm ball)
- L01-RF8: p. 19 allows three-letter symbols, and the p. 18 figure shows Uut/Uup/Uus/Uuo. These are dated: IUPAC approved Nh, Mc, Ts and Og on 28 Nov 2016. → Content says one or two letters; nothing tests three-letter symbols. (IUPAC)
- L01-RF9: the Figure 5 caption (p. 18) says Li, Na and K all "burn" on contact with water, while p. 14 says lithium only fizzes. → Questions use the p. 14 account, reactivity Li < Na < K, with sodium that "can" start a fire (Q15, Q17, Q24); no procedures. Round 1: Q17 extends the trend to rubidium, which the textbook does not describe, so it carries outside evidence. (RSC Education; RSC Periodic Table, rubidium; OpenStax Chemistry 2e §18.1)
- L01-RF10: the spread's tile colours have no legend and are inconsistent (Tb blue and Lu pink, though both are solids). → Colours are never used. (NIH PubChem)
- L01-RF11: pp. 10 and 16 say "everything in the world" is made of atoms, and the brief asks for a matter vs non-matter mission. → Content says all *matter* is made of atoms; Q02 classifies light and sound as non-matter. (OpenStax §1.2; NASA Science, Anatomy of an Electromagnetic Wave)
- L01-RF12: OCR is garbled or empty on pp. 8, 11 (exponents), 15, 16–17 and 18. → All pages rendered; values read from renders (listed above and in the flag).
