## Review flags and resolutions
- L15-RF1 (pp. 184, 186, 187): p. 187 says petroleum takes "hundreds of years" to form, which
  contradicts p. 184's "millions of years". → Game says millions of years, from mostly marine remains.
  Since round 1, no question uses "hundreds", even as a distractor (Q15's recall-trap choice was
  replaced). (EIA)
- L15-RF2 (p. 187): the claim that making ethanol takes more energy than it releases is stated as fact.
  USDA found a positive balance (ratio 1.24 in 1995), and a later study found the gain had grown
  substantially by 2008. → Q16 asks how to test the claim and dates both findings. Q14 says only that
  farming and processing use a lot of energy. (USDA ERS AER-721; DOE EERE; Gallagher, Yee and Baumes
  2016)
- L15-RF3 (p. 187): fermentation is oversimplified. Corn starch is first broken into glucose, and yeast
  ferment it into ethanol plus CO₂. → The O1 card (since round 1) and Q17 teach the starch step; the
  glossary, O1 card, Q17 and Q18 teach the yeast step. The author's record wrongly said the glossary
  covered starch. (DOE EERE; RSC)
- L15-RF4 (pp. 183, 184, 193): the summary defines synthetic as "does not occur in nature", but synthetic
  sapphire is identical to natural sapphire. → Synthetic is defined by how a material is made. Q02's stem
  and explanation carry the repair; since round 2 its distractors target "synthetic means fake" and
  "synthetic means made from petroleum". (GIA)
- L15-RF5 (p. 193): the printed section 5 summary sentence is missing words. → The game uses its own
  summary (key idea 7).
- L15-RF6 (p. 189): the diabetes sentence overstates ("without having to worry about high blood sugar").
  → No medical or diet advice; Q20 uses a sports-drink scenario. (FDA)
- L15-RF7 (pp. 190–191): the book calls paclitaxel a "synthetic material". It is a natural compound
  first extracted from bark; the needle route is semisynthesis from a precursor. → Game wording and a
  Q21 distractor; today's methods are in RF19. (NCI; FSU)
- L15-RF8 (p. 191): the six-trees and 100-years figures are rough estimates, and the Pacific yew also
  grows inland, not only along the coast. → Q22 labels the textbook's estimate; the game makes no
  range claim. (UW Plant Data Sheet; NCI)
- L15-RF9 (p. 190): aspirin is not simply "made from" willow. Chemists derived and synthesized it from
  salicylic acid. → No player-facing text mentions aspirin (checked by search in round 2; the old
  resolution wrongly said game text used it); Q30 uses penicillin and paclitaxel. (NLM)
- L15-RF10 (pp. 183, 193): PLA is called biodegradable without conditions, and the page ends with recycling
  advice. PLA breaks down only in commercial composting and should not go in recycling bins. → The
  O4 watch-out, Q08 and Q28; no disposal advice. Timing is in RF17, bio-based vs biodegradable in RF18.
  (UF/IFAS)
- L15-RF11 (brief guardrail): natural ≠ safe, synthetic ≠ harmful. → Key idea 8, the O3 card, Q07 and
  Q08, and distractors in Q05, Q19 ("natural is always better"), Q20, Q29, Q30 and Q31 ("synthetic can't
  break down"). (NCCIH)
- L15-RF12 (pp. 185, 186): gasoline is called "synthetic", petroleum is said to be "mined", and ethylene
  is said to be "separated out". Refining both separates and cracks. → Gasoline and ethylene are kept
  out of the sorting questions; Q01 says crude oil is "pumped". Since round 2, the natural-resource
  glossary entry and Q01's explanation no longer say a resource stays unchanged "even if it is refined
  later". (EIA refining process)
- L15-RF13 (pp. 182, 185, 187, 188): OCR garbled the formulas and missed the image-only pages. →
  Formulas were read from the renders and match PubChem. (PubChem)
- L15-RF14 (pp. 183, 192): p. 192 lists "inexpensive" among the criteria, while NGSS treats cost as a
  constraint. → Q06 and Q23 state cost as a fixed budget limit. Since round 2, the O4 card, Q24 rationale b
  and Q25's stem no longer list cheapness among the criteria. (NGSS 3-5-ETS1-1)
- L15-RF15 (p. 184), new in round 1: the textbook says PLA bottles bend or squeeze without breaking. Neat
  PLA is stiff and fairly brittle (about 6% elongation at break vs about 130% for PET). → Hook, key
  idea 4, the O2 card and objective O2 now speak of plastics in general and note that PLA is fairly
  stiff; Q09 was recast to plastics. (Kfoury et al. 2013, Frontiers in Chemistry)
- L15-RF16 (p. 187), new: the textbook says fuel corn cannot be used as livestock feed. Ethanol plants
  return distillers grains, a major feed. → Game says fuel corn competes with food and feed (key idea 5,
  O3 card, Q14 i3), and the O3 card mentions distillers grains. (USDA ERS Amber Waves 2019)
- L15-RF17 (p. 193), new: "heated for several days" understates industrial composting, which takes weeks
  to months (ASTM: 90% of the carbon to CO₂ within 180 days). → O4 card, Q08 and Q28 say PLA breaks down
  quickly only when "kept hot for weeks at an industrial composting facility" (the O4 card gained "quickly"
  in round 2). (UF/IFAS)
- L15-RF18 (p. 193), new: the page implies plant-based means biodegradable. → Biodegradable is defined by
  breakdown by living things; Q27 choice a targets the mix-up. (UF/IFAS)
- L15-RF19 (p. 191), new: the needle method is presented as the better way, but yew cell culture later
  replaced the leaves and twigs. → Q03's rationale says "the later needle method", never "newer". Since
  round 2 the O3 card says that by 2004 one drug company was making paclitaxel from yew cells grown in tanks.
  The claim is tied to the date of the EPA page rather than stated as a present-day fact. (EPA 2004 Green
  Chemistry award)
- L15-RF20 (p. 184), new: the page calls coal a mineral and implies water comes from the air. → The game
  does neither. (Geosciences LibreTexts, Organic Sedimentary Rocks)
- L15-RF21 (pp. 192–193), new in round 2: the textbook's goal is a plastic that breaks down quickly in a
  landfill. Landfills soon run short of oxygen, and the bacteria that break waste down there give off methane,
  so fast landfill breakdown is not a plain environmental good. → Q28's limitation and explanation and the O4
  card say so. No question rewards landfill breakdown beyond the lesson's stated design criterion. (EPA LMOP,
  "Basic Information about Landfill Gas")
