## Review flags and resolutions
- **L09-RF1** (p. 111)
  - Issue: the text says the Burgess Shale has yielded "over 60,000 different species". That is the scale of specimen counts, not species.
  - Resolution: no species count is used. Q15 tests why the site matters, which is that it preserves soft-bodied animals.
  - Sources: Royal Ontario Museum (about 200 species, over 150,000 specimens); UCMP "The Cambrian Period".
- **L09-RF2** (pp. 105, 112)
  - Issue: the end-Permian toll appears as 95 percent of lifeforms on p. 105 and 90 percent of species on p. 112. Outside sources also differ.
  - Resolution: content says "the great majority, mostly ocean species", and no question keys a percentage. The O3 card names the disagreement.
  - Sources: NASA "The Great Dying" (about 9 in 10 marine and 7 in 10 land species); NHM London (more than 95 percent).
- **L09-RF3** (pp. 107, 116–117)
  - Issue: "only about 10 percent" for the Phanerozoic conflicts with the textbook's own numbers: 541 ÷ 4,600 ≈ 12 percent. The 541 Ma date is also out of date (538.8 Ma on ICS v2024/12).
  - Resolution: Q05 keys 12 percent (tolerance 0.5), and its limitations note both the textbook's rounding and the 538.8 Ma date. Elsewhere 541 Ma is kept as the lesson's value.
  - Sources: NPS "Geologic Time Scale" and "The Precambrian" (Precambrian about 88 percent); ICS chart.
- **L09-RF4** (p. 107)
  - Issue: "Most of the rock that makes up Earth's crust formed during the current eon" is questionable. The cores of the continents (cratons) are Precambrian. The Phanerozoic gets more chart space mainly because of its fossil record.
  - Resolution: Q06 keys the fossil-record reason, and the crust claim is not used.
  - Sources: USGS Youth and Education "Introduction"; NPS "The Precambrian"; USGS "Age of the Earth".
- **L09-RF5** (p. 110)
  - Issue: the text says cyanobacteria were "the first organisms to carry out photosynthesis" and equates stromatolites with cyanobacteria colonies. Other microbes photosynthesized earlier without releasing oxygen, stromatolites are built by microbial mats in general, and the 3.5 Ga start of oxygen release is debated.
  - Resolution: content says "first organisms known to release oxygen by photosynthesis". Q12 tests only the order of steps.
  - Sources: OpenStax Biology 2e 22.1; UCMP "Cyanobacteria: Fossil Record".
- **L09-RF6** (pp. 111, 116)
  - Issue: the photo caption says all modern organisms trace back to the Cambrian Explosion. Its duration is also inconsistent (about 11 million years in the text, 541–539 Ma on the spread), and 541 Ma is out of date.
  - Resolution: content describes early relatives of most major animal groups appearing long after life began. Q14 repairs the "life began then" idea, and no question keys a duration.
  - Sources: OpenStax Biology 2e 27.4 and 22.1; ICS chart.
- **L09-RF7** (pp. 112–113)
  - Issue: the text says dinosaurs "dominated" from 230 Ma, and that the "Age of the Dinosaurs" ended at 66 Ma. In fact dinosaurs became dominant only after the end-Triassic extinction, and birds are living dinosaurs.
  - Resolution: content says "non-bird dinosaurs" for the 66 Ma extinction, and no question keys the 164-million-year figure.
  - Sources: NHM London (Triassic page, birds page, mass-extinction page).
- **L09-RF8** (pp. 114, 118–119)
  - Issue: "The current ice age began 2.6 million years ago" conflicts with the lesson's own definition (lasting polar ice), because Antarctica has had an ice cap since about 34 Ma (p. 114). The interglacial start of 11,500 years differs from the ICS Holocene base of 11,700 years.
  - Resolution: content says the cycles of large northern ice sheets began about 2.6 Ma. Q22 keys no start date, and Q28 says "big ice sheets begin cycling".
  - Sources: NOAA Climate.gov; ICS chart.
- **L09-RF9** (p. 118)
  - Issue: the Great Lakes are explained only by the weight of ice sinking the crust.
  - Resolution: Q23 and the O3 card add the ice's gouging and deepening of the land, with meltwater filling the basins afterward.
  - Source: NOAA "Great Lakes ecoregion".
- **L09-RF10** (p. 106)
  - Issue: Figure 1A labels the top of the Grand Canyon sequence as 208 Ma. NPS gives about 270 Ma for the Kaibab Formation, the rim rock.
  - Resolution: no question uses Figure 1A's ages. Q24 and Q25 use invented canyons to test the method (superposition plus lateral continuity), which the figure shows correctly.
  - Source: NPS Grand Canyon geology.
- **L09-RF11** (pp. 105, 108–109)
  - Issue: 4.6 billion years is the rounded age of the solar system. USGS gives 4.54 billion years for Earth.
  - Resolution: content dates the solar system to about 4.6 Ga and says Earth was layered by about 4.5 Ga.
  - Source: USGS "Age of the Earth".
- **L09-RF12** (pp. 118–119)
  - Issue: the text says modern humans appeared "more than 200,000 years ago". That is true but out of date: the Smithsonian gives about 300,000 years.
  - Resolution: no question keys this date. Q19's statement c is false under either date.
  - Source: Smithsonian Human Origins "Homo sapiens".
- **L09-RF13** (pp. 108–109, 112–113, 117)
  - Issue: the textbook contradicts itself on two points. Figure 2 says young Earth was "entirely liquid" while the text says "almost completely molten". The text dates the start of Pangaea's breakup to about 200 Ma, while the timeline and spread give 180 Ma.
  - Resolution: content says "largely molten" and "roughly 200 to 180 million years ago", and no question keys either detail.
  - Sources: textbook pages only.
- **L09-RF14** (pp. 104, 107, 116–117)
  - Issue: OCR condition, as described under Source reading.
  - Resolution: all chart and spread content was read from the renders.
