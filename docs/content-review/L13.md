## Review flags and resolutions
- L13-RF1 (p. 169, brief-flagged): the spread says that in most reactions "atoms break apart" and
  then swap. Bonds break, not atoms, and "most reactions" is unsupported. The spread also draws the
  ionic solids CaCO₃ and CaCl₂ as single units, shows hydrochloric acid as loose HCl molecules, and
  uses a doctor/antacid framing. Its atom tallies are correct. → Game text always says bonds break
  and whole atoms regroup; Q10 repairs the wording; the equation is used only as a counting model,
  with no medical context. Q29 uses it to show a mix of patterns (a swap plus a break-up), judged by
  counting substances, with no claim about what "most" reactions do. (OpenStax Chemistry 2e 21.2
  and 3.1; Chemistry LibreTexts 2.1.4 for the carbonic-acid step in Q29.)
- L13-RF2 (p. 166): the printed equation reads "4Fe + 3O₂ → 2F₂O₃" ("F" is fluorine). The figure
  label correctly says 2Fe₂O₃. → Use 4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃ everywhere. Round 1 added: Fe₂O₃ is the
  textbook's and RSC's model product; real burned steel wool can contain other iron oxides (black
  Fe₃O₄ when oxygen is plentiful), so no question asks which exact oxide forms. (RSC Education, "The
  combustion of iron wool"; University of Oxford VRChemistry, "Foundations to Chemistry" section 11
  problems.)
- L13-RF3 (p. 165): the text says a coefficient counts molecules. For the extended structures in
  this lesson (Fe₂O₃, PbI₂, KNO₃, CaCl₂) it counts formula units, and coefficients are relative
  amounts. → Glossary and Explorer text say "molecules or formula units"; Q04, Q06, Q14 and Q29
  state the limitation. (OpenStax Chemistry 2e 4.1 and 3.1.)
- L13-RF4 (p. 164): "twice as much gas" in the hydrogen tube means twice the volume at the same
  temperature and pressure, not twice the mass. → Game says "twice the volume"; Q19 tests volume
  versus mass with conservation of mass. (Temple University Chemistry Demonstrated, "Hoffmann
  Electrolysis".)
- L13-RF5 (p. 163): the opener explains rust as iron plus oxygen only. Rust is a hydrated iron(III)
  oxide, and rusting needs both oxygen and water. → Q20 says oxygen and water from the surroundings.
  (OpenStax Chemistry 2e 17.6.)
- L13-RF6 (p. 168): the text says matter is conserved in "any other changes in matter". → Game text
  limits this to ordinary chemical and physical changes and treats nuclear changes as a separate
  topic. No nuclear questions are asked. Round 1 also removed "always" from the scientific-law
  glossary entry, key idea 6, the O3 card and Q17's key. (OpenStax Chemistry 2e 21.1 and 21.2.)
- L13-RF7 (p. 170): the Figure 4 caption says 75%, but the text and the figure fraction say 77%. The
  green-method beaker is labeled 256 g, but the fraction uses 266 g, and 206 g + 60 g = 266 g. → Use
  266 g and 77% (206 ÷ 266 ≈ 0.774), and 514.5 g and 40% for the 1960s process. EPA and OpenStax
  round the three-step figure to about 80%. (EPA 1997 Greener Synthetic Pathways Award; OpenStax
  Chemistry 2e 4.4.)
- L13-RF8 (p. 170): "efficiency" is said to come from weighing reactants and products. It is
  actually atom economy, a theoretical mass percentage taken from the balanced equations. → Game
  defines it as the share of reactant mass ending up in wanted products (a by-product that gets used
  counts as wanted), separate from reaction speed and actual yield. Q26 and Q28 test this.
  (OpenStax Chemistry 2e 4.4.)
- L13-RF9 (p. 171): the "second solution" story, the polyvinyl alcohol use and the award count are
  textbook narrative. EPA describes acetic acid recovery as part of the commercial BHC process
  (since 1992), but does not mention polyvinyl alcohol. → Questions test only the verified facts:
  three catalytic steps instead of six, and about 99% of reactant mass in useful products when the
  recovered acetic acid is counted. Round 1 corrected the O4 card, its bar chart and the efficiency
  glossary entry, which had said the 99% ended up "as ibuprofen". Objective O4 and Q25 no longer
  present the two-solution history as fact; Q30 teaches the design strategy with hypothetical
  data. (EPA 1997 award page.)
- L13-RF10 (pp. 164, 165, 166, 169): the lesson shows explosive hydrogen, peroxide foaming on skin,
  burning steel wool and an antacid taken for stomach pain. → These appear only as conceptual
  models and balance scenarios: no procedures, no first-aid or medicine advice, and no balloon
  setups in mass questions (buoyancy). Q14's burning-magnesium equation is likewise a counting
  model only.
- L13-RF11 (p. 167, added in round 1): the swap figure draws potassium nitrate as potassium bonded
  to a nitrate group. In water, soluble ionic substances stay dissolved as separate ions (K⁺, NO₃⁻);
  only lead iodide comes out as a solid. → "Swapping partners" is treated as bookkeeping of where
  atoms end up; Q11 and Q29 classify by substance counts only. (OpenStax Chemistry 2e 4.1,
  "Equations for Ionic Reactions".)
