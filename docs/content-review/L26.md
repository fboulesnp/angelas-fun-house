## Review flags and resolutions
- **L26-RF1** (pp. 342–343, 345, 347–354)

  - Resolution: every page was rendered and read. Map patterns, the Figure 4B table, the DART labels and the 1,000–6,000 m depth range, the three Figure 5B fixes and the Figure 7 legend all come from the renders.
- **L26-RF2** (p. 348)
  - Issue: the text says tsunamis "can be accurately predicted" because they have precursor events.
  - Resolution: content separates forecasts made after a triggering event from predicting the next event. Q17 targets the overstatement, and since round 1 its explanation also says what the lesson's "precursor events" are. Q16 shows that a nearby source leaves little time.
  - Sources: NOAA, Tsunami FAQ.
- **L26-RF3** (pp. 346–347)
  - Issue: the text says the subducting plate moves down and drags the water down. USGS describes the upper plate springing up and lifting the seafloor and the water above it.
  - Resolution: content says the seafloor "shifts up or down", and the O2 card names the uplift. No question asks which plate moves or in which direction.
  - Sources: USGS, Earthquake starts tsunami; NOAA, Tsunami FAQ.
- **L26-RF4** (p. 353)
  - Issue: the text gives Harvey "hundreds of billions" in damage and about 60 deaths. NOAA estimates $125 billion (range $90–160 billion) and at least 68 direct deaths.
  - Round 1 reworded the storm-surge sentence. The text calls storm surge the greatest hurricane threat, which matches NHC's general guidance. Harvey was an exception: all but three direct deaths came from freshwater flooding.
  - Resolution: Harvey appears only as a runoff example, including Q22's new stalled-hurricane item. No dollar or death figure is tested, and surge is not ranked above rain flooding.
  - Sources: NHC, Harvey report; NWS Houston/Galveston; NHC, Storm Surge Overview (added in round 1).
- **L26-RF5** (p. 349)
  - Issue: sea walls are presented as simply blocking waves. In 2011, walls and breakwaters were overtopped. The damaged Kamaishi breakwater still cut inundation height by about 40%.
  - Resolution: Q28 evaluates a plan that relies only on a wall. The keyed answer pairs the wall with warnings and plans, and the distractors cover both "safe, nothing more needed" and "a waste".
  - Round 1 narrowed the "damaged defenses" claims in the O4 card and in Q28's rationale b to the one documented structure, Kamaishi.
  - Sources: PARI/NEHRP, Tsunami Resilient Ports.
- **L26-RF6** (pp. 346, 349, 353, 354)

  - Resolution: none of these totals is tested. Q24 uses an invented county count.
  - Sources: PARI/NEHRP (about 40 m run-up and about 19,000 casualties in 2011).
- **L26-RF7** (p. 349)
