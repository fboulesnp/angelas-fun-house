## Review flags and resolutions
- **L07-RF1** (pp. 77, 78, 84): Earth called closed with "none" of its matter entering or leaving.
  - The same p. 78 lists exceptions, and Earth is open to energy.
  - Resolution: content says "nearly closed for matter, open to energy". Q03 separates matter from energy, and Q04 explains why the model stays useful.
  - Sources: NASA Asteroid Fast Facts (more than 100 tons of dust per day); NASA Earth Observatory atmospheric escape (several hundred tons per day); NASA Earth Fact Sheet (mass 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg).
- **L07-RF2** (p. 79): "water molecules a dinosaur drank".
  - Atoms persist, but molecules are split in photosynthesis and rebuilt in respiration.
  - Resolution: Q07 keys the atom-level revision, and the O2 card watchOut says the same.
  - Sources: OpenStax Biology 2e 8.2 and 7.4.
- **L07-RF3** (pp. 79, 84): Figure 2 labels ice → vapor "Evaporation", which is really sublimation.
  - The spread says "water gas" and has a blue arrow labeled "Photosynthesis" between the cloud and soil boxes.
  - Resolution: Q09 keys sublimation, and the evaporation distractor explains the label. Q08 describes clouds as droplets. The spread is cited for its text boxes only.
  - Sources: USGS Water Science School pages on sublimation and condensation.
- **L07-RF4** (p. 82): lightning's products "fall to Earth as ammonia".
  - Lightning makes nitrogen oxides, which reach the ground in rain as nitrates.
  - Resolution: no question says lightning makes ammonia. The O3 card and Q19's rationale say nitrates.
  - Sources: NASA Aura lightning NOx page; EarthDate "Lightning Strikes".
- **L07-RF5** (p. 82): "only one process … two ways" leaves out industrial (Haber–Bosch) fixation.
  - Resolution: Q19 keys fertilizer factories as a third way, and the O3 card mentions them.
  - Source: UF/IFAS EDIS SS684.
- **L07-RF6** (p. 83): "you use the nitrates in the peanut" to make your proteins.
  - Animals use amino acids from digested protein.
  - Resolution: Q24 keys amino acids and carries the nitrate claim as a distractor. The O3 card watchOut covers it too.
  - Source: OpenStax Biology 2e 34.2.
- **L07-RF7** (p. 82): "in nearly all ecosystems … nitrates limit" growth.
  - Phosphorus often limits aquatic systems, and plants take up ammonium too.
  - Resolution: content says "often". Q20 states the soil is nitrogen-poor and tests only what one trial supports. The O3 diagram shows roots absorbing both forms.
  - Sources: OpenStax Biology 2e 46.3; UF/IFAS.
- **L07-RF8** (pp. 79–81, 85): exhaling is treated as cellular respiration, and the manatee's exhaled CO₂ is shown going mostly into the river.
  - Resolution: wording keeps respiration (in cells) separate from breathing (carrying it out). Q10 says "breathes out at the surface", and no item asks where a manatee's breath CO₂ ends up.
  - Sources: USFWS Florida manatee; OpenStax 7.4.
- **L07-RF9** (p. 81): fossil fuels form "under layers of soil".
  - They actually form under sediment and rock, with heat and pressure.
  - Resolution: Q15 and the O4 card use the corrected wording.
  - Sources: EIA "Coal explained"; NASA Earth Observatory "The Carbon Cycle".
- **L07-RF10** (pp. 86–87): the Figure 5 map has no date or source, the "six places" activity can't be reproduced, and the section never says stored forest carbon can be lost again.
  - Resolution: Q27 uses an invented site table instead of the map. Q17 (wildfire) and the O4 watchOut cover the permanence limit.
  - Source: NASA Earth Observatory "The Carbon Cycle".
- **L07-RF11** (p. 83): decomposers said to leave "nitrates and ammonia".
  - Decomposition releases ammonium, and nitrifying bacteria then make the nitrates.
  - Resolution: Q21 matches decomposers to ammonia.
  - Source: UF/IFAS EDIS SS684.
